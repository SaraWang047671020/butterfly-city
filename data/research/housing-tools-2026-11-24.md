### 1d. Most defensible capacity figure per year (recommendation)

- **Taipei: about 3,000–3,500 units started per year, upper bound.** Basis: (i) the central plan's 2nd revision allocated 26,000 contract-awarded units over 8 years, about 3,250/yr (S1). (ii) Ko's stated maximum of about 10,000 units under construction, with a roughly 3-year build, implies about 3,300 starts/yr (derived). (iii) Actual starts recently were much lower: 1,497 in 2023–25, about 500/yr (S11). The remaining pipeline is only about 6,800–7,000 potential units (S4, S5). The game could set the cap at about 3,250/yr, and limit it further by the remaining land pipeline after about 2023.
- **New Taipei: about 4,000 units started per year, upper bound.** Basis: the 33,000/8-year allocation gives about 4,125/yr (S1). 12,954 units are under construction now (S7); with a 3–4-year build, that implies about 3,200–4,300 starts/yr recently (derived). New Taipei also has the larger planning pipeline (16,118 units, S7).
- Why these figures: the allocations are official, city-specific, multi-year commitments negotiated against land availability. They are not a multiple of history, and they agree with the under-construction stock. Caveat: the allocations count *contract award*, not *construction start*. Award usually comes before start, so per-year starts lag.
